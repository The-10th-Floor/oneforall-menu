Order first, then drive.

The decision, the three working parts running, the build, what it costs, what it was checked against, and what nobody has measured yet. One page, start to finish.

The decision

The food takes ten minutes, so the car cannot wait for it

A lane serves one car at a time. Cooking at the window clears about six cars an hour and the queue reaches the road. With the food already made, the same lane handles a car every forty seconds.

Ruled out

The front

A raised terrace with a planted, lit kerb runs the whole frontage, so no car reaches that wall. The apron also dead-ends into parking.

Ruled out

BLK’s passage

The covered lane through the ground floor is theirs. A coffee order clears in a minute and a burger takes ten, so sharing it would stop their queue.

Chosen

The right-hand side

Already paved and lit, the kitchen wall faces it, and it runs through to the back. It is also the route already driven, so a car is known to fit.

01 · The order

A collection time is a cap on the grill

Not a convenience for the customer. Each five-minute slot holds what the grill can actually turn out, and the page will not sell the one after that.

Book a burgerlive

Working demo on the real menu and prices. The cook clock runs at 15× so the sequence is watchable.

Try it. Add two burgers and the times appear with what is left on the grill in each one. Two slots are already full and will not take the order.

Book, then press On my way. The ticket moves onto the kitchen board beside it, and not before.

Nothing else is asked for. Not a name, not a phone number, not the car. The food is handed through a window to the one car standing at it, so five characters on the screen are the whole hand-over. Anything more is a field to fill in that nobody reads back.

Not asked a name Not asked a phone number Not asked the car Not built numbered bays

Grill capacity is set here at eight per five minutes as a placeholder. That number comes from the real line, and it is the single most important setting in the whole system.

02 · The kitchen

Nothing cooks until the customer is coming

A booking holds a place. It does not start a burger. The ticket enters the cook queue on one tap from the customer, which is what keeps a ten-minute item off the lane.

Kitchen boardthe queue

Three states, and the board only ever shows these: waiting for the customer's signal, cooking, and ready to hand over.

If the signal never comes, the slot sends it. The grill place was booked and held either way, so an order starts at its collection time whether or not the customer presses anything. The button only ever makes it earlier. Nobody drives in to find nothing.

The same board tells the kitchen what is coming in the next slots, so the line can be staffed against a number instead of a guess.

A web page cannot track a customer in the background on a phone, so the signal has to be a button. That is the ceiling, not a shortcut, and it is the same signal the large chains pay location tracking to get.

The build

One opening, one bay, two signs

The numbers a builder cannot change afterwards, written down before anyone quotes so the quotes can be compared.

A serving window in the east wallone opening
  • Sill 810–915 mm above the surface the car drives on
  • Opening about 356 × 737 mm. Confirm which figure is the width against the unit’s own drawing, and check a full order passes through
  • Driver’s side, confirmed. Jordan drives on the right, so the driver sits left and the wall is on the car’s left
  • An air curtain over the opening, and a shutter that closes between cars and at night
  • A hand basin within reach on the kitchen side
  • No speaker, no screen, no menu board. The order arrives before the car does
A pull-in bay, off the linepaint

Three metres by six, against the boundary wall, past the window and clear of the chargers. A car whose food is not ready pulls in, stays in, and drives out forwards. It is not a parking space. A second bay depends on the corridor width.

Two signsreflective or lit

One at the entry, left to park and right to the drive-through, and one marking the pull-in bay. Both reflective or internally lit: the night footage shows a great deal of competing light on that forecourt, and a printed panel disappears into it. The bay needs no number, because nobody has to read it back to us.

Carrying orders out on foot needs no permission and no builder, but it is a wage every month for as long as the lane runs. The window is paid once. If the volume needs a dedicated runner, the window costs less than one from about the second month. If an existing counter member can walk it out, the window buys shelter and distance rather than a saving, and it can wait.

The evidence

What this was checked against

Four research passes run in parallel: operator precedent, what customers actually do, real costs in Jordan, and how this fails elsewhere. The findings that argue against the plan are here too.

Taking the order out of the lane

Chipotle’s pickup lane has no menu board at all. Pay ahead, arrive, hand over in under thirty seconds. Their stated reason is that a speaker box slows throughput and costs margin. Stores with one run about 15% higher sales.

QSR Magazine · Chipotle newsroom

Cook-to-order burgers in a lane

Smashburger serves smashed burgers with no lane at all. Order on the phone, pull into a marked bay, a team member carries it out with a stated wait. They chose it so a conventional lane would not be needed.

Smashburger, March 2023

Does anyone actually order ahead?

Shake Shack, the closest burger comparison, ran 37.0% digital across 2025. Chipotle 36.7%. A dedicated pickup lane is the strongest lever measured, worth about 80% more order-ahead volume.

Company results, full year 2025

The case against, which is real

Starbucks is retiring its pickup-only stores, calling the format too transactional. In-N-Out runs no app at all, deliberately, and thrives. Cancellations cost restaurants 1.5–3% of digital sales a week.

Restaurant Dive · Restaurant Business

And the number nobody has

No published no-show rate exists for restaurant order-ahead pickup, in any market. The timing this depends on has never been measured by anyone, which is why the first weeks should be counted rather than assumed.

Searched, not found

Jordan, on the one input that matters

Internet penetration was 92.5% at the start of 2025, and ordering through a web page rather than an app is already normal locally. The menu page, the tap piece and the loyalty card already exist.

Known failures

What goes wrong, and what stops it

Every row has happened to somebody. Four of the six are settled by a decision taken before any work starts, which is the reason to take them now.

Order accuracy is the first thing operators name when a drive-through gets busy, ahead of staffing, and one person covering both a counter and a window is where it slips. The answer is not a hire. It is the collection slot, which caps how many orders the grill is asked for in five minutes.
